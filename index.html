Forecasting — the business case

Built from your SAP data
Recent sales Same season, last year Festivals & sale days New / popular / slow-moving
Two methods, for two different businesses

Batteries — a steady, trend-based method

  • High volume — even a small forecast error means a lot of excess or shortfall stock
  • Demand varies by zone — driven by local conditions and channel mix
  • Different baseline demand across zones
  • A simple trend-based method gets this right consistently — manual effort doesn't

Lighting — a smarter, event-aware method

  • Close to 500 products, with new ones launching often
  • Sourced from China — long wait if the forecast is wrong
  • Demand jumps sharply around festivals like Diwali
  • New products have no sales history to learn from

Forecasting — the technical model

Segment before picking a model
Batteries — mature demand Lighting — fast-fashion-adjacent
Mathematical Model Used
Stable, high-volume (most Batteries)
Classical time-series
Exponential smoothing (Holt-Winters / ETS)
E.g. AA batteries sell ~40,000 units/month, steady for 3 years straight.
Seasonal, high-volume (festival Lighting)
Time-series + event modelling
Gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM/XGBoost-style)
E.g. LED string lights sell 5x more in the 3 weeks before Diwali.
E.g. that same demand drops roughly 80% in the 2 weeks right after Diwali, once the festival season ends.
Long-tail slow movers
Intermittent-demand method
Croston's method / TSB
E.g. A niche torch model sells only 20-30 units, once every 10-15 days.
New launches, zero history
Cold-start, comparable-SKU method
Nearest-neighbour on category, price tier, channel mix
E.g. A new 9W LED bulb has no sales history — it copies last year's closest-priced launch's first-3-month curve.
NoteThese are directional. Once run on your real SAP data, the model per segment may change based on how each actually performs.

Reorder planning

Beyond the forecast
Current stock Open purchase orders Vendor lead time Approved forecast
From forecast to action — reorder planning

What it does

  • Combines the approved forecast with current depot stock, any open POs already in transit, and vendor lead time
  • Recommends exactly what to reorder, how much, and by when — not just a number to interpret
  • Flags urgency automatically — Order Now / Order Soon / Healthy — so nothing falls through the cracks
  • Works across zones and depots at once, not one spreadsheet per person

Why it matters

  • Removes the manual step of translating a forecast into a purchase decision
  • Closes the gap where the forecast was right but nobody reordered in time
  • The same accuracy gains from better forecasting flow straight into production and procurement planning
  • Bulk actions mean approving reorder quantities takes minutes, not days, even across thousands of SKUs and depots

Stock imbalance & transfer planning

Depot-to-depot, same region
Depot-level stock Same-region transfers Excess vs. shortage
When one depot has excess and another falls short

What it does

  • Watches stock levels at every depot, not just the zone total
  • Flags when one depot is sitting on excess while a nearby depot in the same region is running short
  • Recommends a specific transfer — which depot, which SKU, how many units — not just a report
  • Already live in this prototype — see the Stock Imbalance Transfer tab (Patna → Dhulagarh, East region)

Why it matters

  • Today, if a depot runs short, nobody knows where the nearest surplus is or how quickly it can move
  • Turns "stock is stuck somewhere" into one specific, actionable transfer
  • Reduces excess inventory and stockouts at the same time, without raising a new purchase order
  • Uses the same stock and depot data the forecast already tracks — no separate system needed

How the data reaches EasyReplenish — Option 1: FTP transfer
  1. A scheduled job in SAP writes the day's extracts as flat files.
  2. The files are placed on a secure FTP location, controlled by Eveready's IT team.
  3. EasyReplenish collects the files from that location each night.
  4. Each file is checked — record counts, dates and product codes — before anything is loaded.
  5. If a file fails the check, it is reported back the same morning and nothing part-loaded.
SAP stays the single source of truth. EasyReplenish only reads from it — no SAP data is changed, and no master data is re-created on our side. Approved forecasts can be sent back into SAP later if Eveready wants that.
